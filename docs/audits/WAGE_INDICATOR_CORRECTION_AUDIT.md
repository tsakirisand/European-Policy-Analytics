## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Diagnosis

### 1.1 The Issue
The European Policy Analytics platform previously featured an indicator designated as `AVG_ANNUAL_WAGE` under the Labour domain (`LABOUR`). An audit of data ingestion pipelines and source mappings revealed that `AVG_ANNUAL_WAGE` was fetching data from the World Bank API using series ID:
- **`NY.GDP.PCAP.CD`**: *Gross Domestic Product per capita (current US$)*.

### 1.2 Theoretical and Methodological Discrepancy
- **GDP per capita** represents the total gross value added generated by all resident producers in an economy, divided by the midyear population. It measures overall macroeconomic output and average productivity, not individual compensation or worker earnings.
- Furthermore, `NY.GDP.PCAP.CD` is denominated in **US Dollars ($)** and includes all individuals (children, retirees, unemployed), whereas wage indicators measure compensation paid to full-time equivalent employees, denominated in **Euros (€)** in the European context.
- Storing and displaying GDP per capita under a wage label led to gross distortion of labor market analytics (e.g., in Luxembourg, GDP per capita exceeds $120,000 due to cross-border commuters, which diverges significantly from average domestic full-time wages).

---

## 2. The Official Eurostat Replacement: `nama_10_fte`

### 2.1 Series Identification & Legal Basis
The wage metric has been replaced with the official Eurostat series:
- **Dataset Code:** `nama_10_fte`
- **Official Name:** *Average full-time adjusted salary per employee*
- **ESMS Metadata Citation:** [https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/nama_10_fte_esms.htm](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/nama_10_fte_esms.htm)
- **Legal Context:** Published pursuant to Directive (EU) 2021/1883 (the revised EU Blue Card Directive), which establishes harmonized gross annual salary thresholds for high-skilled migrant workers across EU Member States.

### 2.2 Dataset Dimensions & Technical Details
The dataset is retrieved directly from the Eurostat Dissemination API (`https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/nama_10_fte?lang=en&unit=EUR`):
- **Frequency (`freq`):** `A` (Annual)
- **Unit (`unit`):** `EUR` (Euros per employee per year) / `NAC` (National currency)
- **Indicator Denomination in Platform:** `Euros per year` (Symbol: `€`)
- **Geographic Dimension (`geo`):** Reporting EU Member States and aggregates (`EU27_2020`, `EA20`).
- **Time Dimension (`time`):** Historical coverage spanning from 1995 to 2024.
- **New Indicator ID in Catalog:** `AVG_FULL_TIME_ADJUSTED_SALARY`

---

## 3. Decoupling and Preserving GDP per Capita

To maintain macroeconomic analysis capabilities without conflating output with labor remuneration:
- World Bank series `NY.GDP.PCAP.CD` has been decoupled into its own dedicated indicator:
  - **Indicator Code:** `GDP_PER_CAPITA`
  - **Domain:** `ECON` (Economy)
  - **Source:** World Bank (WDI)
  - **Unit:** `US Dollars` (Symbol: `$`)
  - **Label:** *GDP per Capita (current US$)*
  - **Description:** *Gross domestic product divided by midyear population, in current US dollars.*

## 5. Country Coverage & Data Limitations

### 5.1 EU Member States Coverage
- Eurostat's `nama_10_fte` covers 26 of the 27 EU Member States plus European aggregates (`EU27_2020`, `EA20`).
- **The Netherlands (`NLD`) Exception:** The Netherlands is explicitly omitted from standard publication in `nama_10_fte` tables due to national statistical register methodologies that do not produce directly comparable full-time adjusted employee salary figures under the harmonized Blue Card formula. (Noted in Section 5 of the platform methodology and Eurostat ESMS documentation).
- **Non-EU Countries:** Non-EU countries (e.g., United Kingdom, Switzerland, Norway) do not submit data under the ESA 2010 Blue Card mandate. For these nations, national statistical office baselines (ONS, FSO, SSB) are integrated into seed data to preserve cross-European analytical continuity.
